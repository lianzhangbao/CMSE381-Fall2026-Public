## Course description

CMSE 381 is an introduction course to statistical and machine learning. It covers the fundamentals of data science methods, including unsupervised learning and supervised learning, feature extraction, dimension reduction, clustering, regression and classification.

## Course objectives

Understand how to find patterns and structure in data using statistical methods and machine learning algorithms, and be able to implement simple versions of such techniques. Understand the concepts of unsupervised and supervised learning broadly, in addition to having proficiency in common algorithms and ideas within each field.

## Grading

### Homework

This semester, the course pilots an AI-assisted homework grading system. All homework must be submitted directly to the platform (<https://www.msu-stt-learning.com>) as a Jupyter Notebook (.ipynb) file.

The AI-assisted system is used solely to support grading and feedback. All grades and feedback are reviewed by a TA and/or the instructor before release. Student submissions and data are handled in accordance with FERPA privacy and data protection requirements.

Make sure your submitted notebook runs from top to bottom without errors and that all required code, outputs, and explanations are clearly visible. A clear and well-organized solution pathway is expected.

Academic integrity policies apply. All submitted work must be your own unless collaboration is explicitly permitted. Any use of generative AI tools must be clearly disclosed, with citations for the tools and models used, including the prompts used to generate content.

**Late work.** Homework is due on the days marked in the schedule at midnight.

- Submitted before midnight the following day: graded with a 5% penalty.
- Submitted before midnight two days after the due date: graded with a 15% penalty.
- After that: no credit, but see the missed work policy below.

For example, if homework is due on Friday, work turned in before midnight on Saturday has a 5% penalty, work turned in before midnight on Sunday has a 15% penalty, and nothing is accepted after that.

**Collaboration policy.**

- We **do** assume you will talk to each other to work on things.
- We **do** assume you will look up definitions while you work.
- We **do** assume you will include an acknowledgement section in your homework naming the people and resources you used. *Example: I worked with Maryam Mirzakhani and Grigori Perelman while completing this assignment. I also used Wikipedia to understand Galois cohomology and Stack Overflow to get me started on an algorithm for enumerating Hamiltonian cycles in a graph.*
- We **do not** assume you will copy each other's work or copy from the internet.

### Quizzes

Once a week there is a short in-class quiz on basics from the previous week's course. It is at the beginning of class and lasts about 10 minutes. Quizzes check your understanding of definitions and interpretations of the content. No makeups are given; see the missed work policy below.

### Exams

There are three non-cumulative midterms. See the schedule for dates. There is no final exam.

### Project

There is a final project where you take a publicly available data set, use tools learned in class to analyze it, and submit a written report. It is due at the end of the semester, with several checkpoints along the way. See the [project description](../Project/ProjectDescription.md).

### Missed work policy

At the end of the semester, we drop the lowest homework grade and the two lowest quiz grades. For this reason, no late homework is accepted beyond the late windows above, and no quiz makeups are available.

If you have an issue (for example, medical or bereavement) that makes you miss a midterm, contact Dr. Bao as soon as possible, preferably before the exam. A time to make up the exam will be organized with you.

## Acceptable and unacceptable use of generative AI

This course is about machine learning, so understanding generative AI tools such as ChatGPT is directly relevant to our learning objectives. These tools are new, and we are all still learning how to use them ethically in our classroom. In class, we will draft a list of examples of how the class thinks these tools can and cannot be used for this course.

Your use of AI tools must be documented and cited to stay within university policies on academic integrity and the Spartan Code of Honor Academic Pledge. **Clearly indicate where generative AI has been used in your assignments and provide citations for the AI tools and models you used, along with the prompts you used to generate content.** You can do this through comments in your code, annotations in your documentation, or a dedicated section in your homework or project reports. Citing both the tools and the prompts gives transparency and reproducibility, and credits the tools' creators.

Any assignment found to have used generative AI tools in unauthorized ways may be given a 0. When in doubt about permitted usage, ask for clarification.

## Academic honesty

Intellectual integrity is the foundation of the scientific enterprise. In all instances, you must do your own work and give proper credit to all sources that you use in your papers and oral presentations. Any instance of submitting another person's work, ideas, or wording as your own counts as plagiarism. This includes failing to cite any direct quotations in your essays, research paper, class debate, or written presentation. The MSU College of Engineering adheres to the policies of academic honesty as specified in the General Student Regulations 1.0, Protection of Scholarship and Grades, and in the all-University statement on Integrity of Scholarship and Grades, which are included in Spartan Life: Student Handbook and Resource Guide. Students who plagiarize will receive a 0.0 in the course. In addition, University policy requires that any cheating offense, regardless of the magnitude of the infraction or punishment decided upon by the professor, be reported immediately to the dean of the student's college. (See also the Academic Integrity webpage.)

Plagiarism in this course includes, but is not limited to, directly copying another student's solutions to assignments; copying materials from online sources, textbooks, or other reference materials without citing those references in your source code or documentation; or having somebody else do your in-class work or homework on your behalf. Any work done in collaboration with other students should say so explicitly and list their names as well as yours.

More broadly, we ask that students adhere to the Spartan Code of Honor academic pledge, as written by the Associated Students of Michigan State University (ASMSU):

> "As a Spartan, I will strive to uphold values of the highest ethical standard. I will practice honesty in my work, foster honesty in my peers, and take pride in knowing that honor is worth more than grades. I will carry these values beyond my time as a student at Michigan State University, continuing the endeavor to build personal integrity in all that I do."
